TROIS-PISTOLES

The history of Trois-Pistoles spans 300 years of settlement. Basque fishermen sailed up the river in the 16th century and landed on the Île aux Basques, that "jewel of the river". Then its capricious currents carried Jean Riou to the shore of the Seigneury of Trois-Pistoles in 1696. 

Trois-Pistoles and the neighbouring villages form the Regional County Municipality of Les Basques. The Île aux Basques lies 5 km off Trois-Pistoles; since 1929 it has belonged to the Provancher Society of Natural History of Canada, which made it a sanctuary for migratory birds. From 1990 to 1993, archaeological digs were carried out there under the auspices of Université Laval, prompted by historical discoveries in the archives of Bordeaux (France). These digs uncovered remains and various objects bearing witness to the Basques' main activity on the island at the turn of the 16th and 17th centuries: rendering the blubber of whales caught nearby. The digs also revealed traces of repeated stays by Indigenous peoples going back at least to the 8th century. There are signs suggesting that the Île aux Basques was the scene of the first trading exchanges between Europeans and Indigenous peoples in the St. Lawrence estuary. These discoveries earned the island recognition as a National Historic Site by the federal government in 2001.

THE BASQUES REGION

The Basques region is a vast territory comprising one town, ten municipalities and one unorganized territory. The town of Trois-Pistoles has the densest population but, on the other hand, covers only 7.74 square kilometres. 

Unlike Trois-Pistoles, the unorganized territory of the Les Basques RCM has no year-round residents but covers a very large area: 115.57 square kilometres, or 10.23% of the total area of the RCM. This territory is used mainly for fishing, hunting and logging.
