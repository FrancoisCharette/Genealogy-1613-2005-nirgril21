ST-PACÔME

A remarkable site on the banks of a salmon river

Saint-Pacôme's charm comes from its setting and its topography. Set a little back from the farming plain along the St. Lawrence, it clings both to the bank of the Rivière-Ouelle and to the side of the marine terrace formed a few thousand years ago when the glaciers melted. Salmon swim up the winding river into the gorges and falls upstream of the village, where anglers come to try their luck and where you can go hiking amid impressive scenery.

The magnificent estate of the King family, who developed the lumber industry and the village's prosperity, bears witness to a rich architecture. 

A few kilometres from the village, the Rivière-Ouelle wharf stands at the end of a bay (the Anse des Mercier), in a pleasant spot with a small beach and a breathtaking view of the capes of Charlevoix.

FOUNDED IN 1851

This municipality, between Saint-Philippe-de-Néri and Sainte-Anne-de-la-Pocatière, lies about 5 kilometres from La Pocatière.

On February 8, 1851, the parish of Saint-Pacôme was canonically established.  When it was founded, the parish of Saint-Pacôme lived almost entirely from logging.  While the mills, both the  sawmills and the flour mill, long provided the people of Saint-Pacôme with a living, today the village has turned to recreation and tourism.  

A very interesting heritage trail takes in nearly 20 houses, the church and a seigneurial mill. 

The church of St-Pacôme was built in 1851, with the carpenter Louis Langelier of St-Pascal directing the work.  Barely finished, it burned down after the first high mass.  It was rebuilt at once on the same spot,  and in 1907 it was enlarged with a transept and porticoes with triangular pediments in the neoclassical style.

The presbytery, built in 1865, is now an inn.

In front of the church, the Côte-Norbert leads to the top of a hill (the Côte-des-Chats), where a lookout lets you admire and take in an exceptional panorama over the whole St. Lawrence plain : the village below, the farmland where the river winds down to the village of Rivière-Ouelle, then the thirty-kilometre width of the river with Île-aux-Coudres and finally, in the distance, the bay of Baie-Saint-Paul on the north shore. The light plays with the clouds above the river, creating patches of sparkling light in perpetual motion.
