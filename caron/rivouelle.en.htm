RIVIÈRE-OUELLE
About ten kilometres from La Pocatière,  Rivière-Ouelle is a village whose main activity is farming, on rich land made up mostly of clay.

On October 29, 1672, Intendant Talon granted Jean-Baptiste Deschamps De Boishébert, Seigneur de la Bouteillerie, a fief two and a half leagues deep along the St. Lawrence River, on each side of the Ouelle River. Clearing of the most fertile land began right away. 

The decree of the King's Council of State of March 1722 mentions "La Bouteillerie", known as Rivière-Ouelle, to designate the place that would be set up in 1855 as the parish municipality of Notre-Dame-de-Liesse-de-la-Rivière-Ouelle. The people of Rivière-Ouelle were nicknamed Capelans and Marsouins (capelin and porpoises) in those days, since they caught these fish in abundance.
