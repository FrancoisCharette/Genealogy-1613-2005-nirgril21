Marie-Anna, and Gérard with his family, move to rue Des Écores

Saint-Barthélemy parish. Marie-Anna then buys a six-unit building and lives on the ground floor; two of her sons and their families have flats in it.

Marie-Anna's address.

Nicole's text
In 1952 Marie-Anna moves to rue Des Écores in Saint-Barthélemy parish. The following year she buys the six-unit building next door to the south and moves into the ground floor, number 7082. Her sons Gérard and Jean-Noël each rent a flat there. The rents are her main income. original page
From the 1952 Lovell* directory we know that Gérard and his family move to 7078 Des Écores and that his mother, Marie-Anna, moves close by, to 7092 rue Des Écores in the parish of St-Barthélemy. Who followed whom? It is the second floor of a house adjoining, to the south, a block of six flats that Marie-Anna buys the following year. She then moves into the north ground-floor flat, no. 7082. In 1954 Jean-Noël moves in next door, at no. 7080. The rents are her main income. original page
From 1946 to 1964 Marie-Anna has twenty-one grandchildren. In the 1950s she lives on the ground floor of a six-unit building that she bought on rue des Écores, in Montréal. Two of her sons and their families live in flats in the same building. Marie-Anna loves anything new and modern and buys it as soon as it comes on the market: a refrigerator, a shower, a TV... She always likes to have the latest model of everything, and her children buy it for her when they go to New York now and then. original page

1957Home

Marie-Anna leaves rue Des Écores for a residence, then Marieville

She keeps the building; Gérard by then lives in Saint-Luc.

Nicole's text
In 1957 Marie-Anna moves into a seniors' residence on boulevard Gouin and, a few years later, into a nursing home in Marieville, as her health declines. Marie-Anna dies there on July 8, 1966, at the age of 69, of cancer of the intestines. original page
From the 1957 Lovell* directory on, we find no more trace of Marie-Anna on rue Des Écores, or anywhere else. We know she owned the building until her death, but she no longer lives there. Nor does Gérard; he lives in St-Luc but goes on working for the City of Montréal. Only Jean-Noël lives there, until 1960 inclusive, after which he moves to Pont-Viau. Note that Gérard's name still appears in some directories; I think that is because he had to give a Montréal address to be allowed to work for the City. He also had to be a Montréal resident to register his children at the Montréal library. original page

October 17, 1964Death

Ézilda Côté dies in her Montreal flat

The emergency call is taken by her son-in-law Gérard Charette, a Montreal police dispatcher. She is buried in a common grave in Côte-des-Neiges cemetery.

Nicole's text
Ézilda died at home on October 17, 1964. She is said to have died of breathing problems after some painting she had taken on alone in her flat, or, according to others, of an aneurysm. It was her grandson Robert Daoust who is said to have raised the alarm. His brother Gilles remembers that it was a very beautiful fall day; he remembers seeing Arsène's car come racing up, as he had just been told of the situation. Arsène worked nights for Canadian National (the railway), so he was at home during the day and could be reached quickly; no doubt he was woken up. original page
As chance would have it, the emergency call (what we now call 911) was taken by Gérard Charette, Ézilda's son-in-law. He worked as an emergency call dispatcher for the Montreal police. His shifts were six hours straight a day, and he "changed shifts" every week. So on October 17, 1964, Gérard was working days and himself sent the emergency services to Ézilda's. But it was too late. Gérard left work to rush to the scene, then went home to St-Luc to break the news in person to Jacqueline. Here is Francine's account: " I remember that that day I was playing alone outside, and it was a nice day. I saw Papa's car as it went by my uncle Benoît's former garage. I ran in to tell Maman that Papa was coming. She told me that was impossible, that he was at work, but it really was him. They went off by themselves and he told her the news." original page
Ézilda had hoped to be buried near her husband, but of course that was not possible. So after her death on October 17, 1964, she was buried in a common grave in the Côte-des-Neiges Catholic cemetery . original page

October 6, 1970Marriage

Antoine (Antonio) Caron, Marie-Anna's brother, marries Éva Lamontagne in Sherbrooke

Nicole's text
Research in the BMS2000 database tells us that Antoine marries Éva Lamontagne on October 6, 1970, in Sherbrooke. She is surely the common-law partner he had lived with for so long. Éva had first married in Sherbrooke in 1921, to George Cross, from England, and had had a daughter with him, Lilianne, who married in 1946 in Montréal. So Antoine and Éva did finally marry, probably when she became a widow; they were each over seventy. Antoine dies in December 1992. original page

Ézilda as a grandmother

Sixteen grandchildren from 1947 to 1964; she helps her daughters after each birth and loves listening to the radio. In the mid-1950s Fabiola and Elphège move into the flat next door.

Nicole's text
From 1947 to 1964 Ézilda has sixteen grandchildren. She still lives on rue Létourneux. She devotedly helps her two daughters through their "relevailles" (the weeks of rest after childbirth) after each birth. Ézilda loves listening to the radio and often goes to the movies in the neighbourhood. She only buys herself a refrigerator or a TV at the very end of her life. Modern gifts don't interest her; she puts them away, still in their wrapping, at the back of the closet. For example, rather than an electric iron, she prefers her old irons, which she heats on her wood stove. original page
In the mid-1950s, Ézilda could count on her daughter Fabiola and her son-in-law Elphège, who moved into the flat next door, at 2175 Létourneux. They lived there with their four children. Elphège worked as a caretaker for the Catholic School Commission. As for Arsène, he spent most of his married life on rue Orléans, also in the Maisonneuve district. Only Jacqueline lived farther from her mother, first in Verdun, then in the north of Montreal, and in St-Luc, a small village in St-Jean county about twenty miles from Montreal. Still, she visited Ézilda very regularly. original page

Gérard, a man of the Quiet Revolution

Nicole's text
Born on October 19, 1921, in Montréal, Gérard is, all his life, a man of his time. He belongs to the generation of men and women who carry out the Quiet Revolution (Révolution tranquille) to give their children a better life and, in Québec, an opening onto the world. Gérard throws himself into the new social movements, often from their very beginnings: the right to education and its importance for everyone, girls as much as boys; the independence movement of the early 1960s; the health trends such as naturopathy, homeopathy, natural foods and therapeutic fasting, etc... Gérard loves driving anything that rolls on four wheels: cars, taxis, trucks, tankers or trailers; he takes part in driving-skill contests and rallies. He loves to keep moving, meet lots of people, belong to groups and associations of every kind, take courses, change jobs, and always have one or two part-time jobs on top of his regular work. Since the latter is often "on shifts", Gérard does not know regular sleep or a regular life, and this for a long time. original page
