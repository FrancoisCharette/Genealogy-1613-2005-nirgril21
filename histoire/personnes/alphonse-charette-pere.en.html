Alphonse Charette Sr.

8th generation. A mason turned contractor and property owner in Saint-Henri; with Rosianne Miron he has fourteen children. His house at 1106 rue Saint-Antoine is where the family gathers.

In Nicole's words

Alphonse Charette is born on September 14, 1850, in Saint-Jérôme, in the Pays d'en-Haut, where his parents, Jacques and Théotiste Dubois, had settled after their marriage. He is the eighth of sixteen children. His godparents are Jean-Baptiste Bériault and Rosalie Charette. Jacques, a farmer and day labourer, often works away from home, since the note "père absent" (father absent) is added to the baptism record of his son Alphonse, and of nearly all his children. Around 1853 the family moves to Saint-Sauveur, and around 1870 to Saint-Henri-des-Tanneries. 8th and 9th generations — The two Alphonses — alph1
Rosianne Miron is born on May 15, 1856, in Montréal, Notre-Dame parish. Her parents, Antoine and Angèle Pichette, had married on August 2, 1841, in Saint-Jean-sur-Richelieu. Her godparents are Michel Larose and Dorothée Miron, the child's paternal uncle and aunt. We know of three brothers of Rosianne, Antoine Jr., Félix and Léonard, and one sister, Philomène. Their father Antoine dies on (...) and their mother Angèle remarries on April 17, 1865, in Montréal, a widower with several children, Louis Casavant. Note that he is also the brother-in-law of Angèle's son: Antoine Miron Jr. had married Julie Casavant, Louis's sister, a year earlier. 8th and 9th generations — The two Alphonses — alph1
On Tuesday, February 25, 1873, in the church of Saint-Joseph parish, rue Richmond in Montréal, Alphonse Charette marries Rosianne Miron. The groom's witness is his father Jacques, and the bride's is her stepfather Louis Casavant. Alphonse is twenty-two and Rosianne sixteen. Neither of them can sign the marriage record. 8th and 9th generations — The two Alphonses — alph1
Alphonse works as a mason. At first he works for others; then, in his mid-forties, he becomes a masonry contractor. The first mention of this appears in the Lovell street directory of 1894. He builds many cut-stone houses, and even churches and other buildings. 8th and 9th generations — The two Alphonses — alph1
He also builds a fine big stone house for his family at 1106 rue Saint-Antoine. They move in in 1895, and little by little it becomes the house of the Pater Familias, a role Alphonse fills happily, since he is clearly very proud of his family. This house is the setting for countless family gatherings to mark the happy and the sad moments. It is said that the basement is specially fitted out; Alphonse even puts in a complete kitchen there to make things easier for the women, who can cook in comfort while staying "with the company". The table is big enough to seat as many people as possible. There is a piano, others play the fiddle, and people have a good time at these parties. They sing, dance and play cards. Alphonse and Rosianne love to gather family and friends around them and to entertain them well. (Story passed on by Ghislaine, who heard it from Rita and Fleurette Lavoie, Rosario's cousins) 8th and 9th generations — The two Alphonses — alph1
In addition to his trade, Alphonse gradually becomes an important property owner in Saint-Henri. He owns several apartment blocks; he looks after managing and maintaining them and the well-being of his tenants. In the marriage records of two of his sons, in 1907, Alphonse is listed as a "bourgeois" (a man of property). In the census of 1911 he declares himself a "rentier" (living on his income). In the same census several of his children, then newly married, are living in flats next to his. It is fair to say that Alphonse and Rosianne, despite their lack of schooling and the hardships they faced, built an enviable family and financial comfort. Alphonse dies on April 25, 1919. In 1928 the city changes the street numbering, and 1106 becomes 4230 rue Saint-Antoine. Rosianne lives there until her death on April 26, 1935. 8th and 9th generations — The two Alphonses — alph1
