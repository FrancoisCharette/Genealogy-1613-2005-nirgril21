Robert Caron

In Nicole's words

Of all our ancestral lines known to date, Robert Caron is the first to arrive in New France, on June 4, 1634 (he was in the first group brought over by Robert Giffard). Marie-Anna Caron and Rosario Charette — caron
Robert Caron, probably born on July 22, 1613, in Rouen, France, sails for New France on one of the four ships of the Compagnie Cheffault-Rozee (a company founded for 5 years, based in Rouen, and charged with carrying immigrants to the St. Lawrence valley). Robert Caron thus arrives with Robert Giffard, seigneur of Beauport, and all the first settlers who came with him: Jean Côté, Jean Bourbon, Noël Langlois, Zacharie Cloutier. Marie-Anna Caron and Rosario Charette — caron
On October 25, 1637, Robert marries Marie Crevet (aged 16), who had arrived in the country in July 1637. She is the daughter of Pierre Crevet and the late Marie Lemercier, of Bénouville, diocese of Bayeux, in Normandy. The wedding takes place in the chapel of Notre-Dame-de-la-Recouvrance. It is blessed by the Jesuit Charles Lallemant, who serves as parish priest in Québec. Jasmin Bourguignon, a friend, Noël Langlois, a settler from the very beginning, and Robert Giffard, surgeon and seigneur of Beauport, attend the wedding as witnesses. Seven children will be born of this union. Marie-Anna Caron and Rosario Charette — caron
In 1642 Robert, fearing the Iroquois, not without reason, sells his land to his neighbour Guillaume Couillard for 150 livres. He settles in Québec on the côteau Sainte-Geneviève (Sainte-Geneviève hillside), later to be called « le Belvédère »; he clears land to make an opening and keep a better eye on the movements of the Iroquois. Timber is his main source of income. Several settlers of Norman origin had settled in this area, which is located where the present-day Collège des Jésuites stands, at the corner of René-Lévesque and Joffre. Marie-Anna Caron and Rosario Charette — caron
On July 8, 1656, Robert Caron dies at the age of 44 at the Hôtel-Dieu. An accident or an illness? Marie-Anna Caron and Rosario Charette — caron
Ten years after Robert Caron's death, on July 27, 1666, Marie Crevet remarries, to Noël Langlois, in Château-Richer. To avoid quarrels between the Langlois and Caron heirs, Marie and Noël sign a marriage contract under "séparation de biens" (separate property). Marie and Noël have known each other for a long time. He was a pilot, born in France in 1603, who came to Canada in 1634 at the same time as Robert Caron. Noël had been a witness at Marie and Robert Caron's wedding. He himself had first married Françoise Garnier, with whom he had ten children. Marie-Anna Caron and Rosario Charette — caron
Joseph Caron, born in 1652 in Québec, settles in the Bas du Fleuve (the lower St. Lawrence). This is because in the autumn of 1672 his younger sister, Aymée, marries one of their stepfather's sons, Noel Langlois fils dit Traversy. Frontenac grants him the Seigneury of Port-Joly. He will sell it in 1686, having been unable to meet the requirements expected of a seigneur, but in the meantime several of his Caron brothers-in-law, including our ancestor Joseph, follow their sister and her husband and move to this new region to be developed, the Bas du Fleuve. Marie-Anna Caron and Rosario Charette — caron
