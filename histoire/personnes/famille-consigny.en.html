In Nicole's words

The son of Jacques Sanfaçon dit Consigny and Jeanne Bijolet, born in France in the diocese of Langres, François married Marie-Anne Ducharme at Notre-Dame de Québec on November 21, 1735. At the Consignys' — arbre_genealogique
François Sanfaçon dit Consigny is said to have been a soldier. The Consignys lived down the river, in St-Jean-Port-Joli, Rivière-Ouelle and St-Pascal, then in Quebec City and Montreal. At the Consignys' — arbre_genealogique
Nicolas Consigny and Basilisse Landry married on May 2, 1843. They had three children. At the Consignys' — arbre_genealogique
Charles's parents, Arsène Consigny and Amanda Thibaudeau, married on July 31, 1888, in Quebec City, in the church of Saint-Roch parish. They were born there, Arsène on October 14, 1865, and Amanda on February 2, 1862, and they would spend their whole lives there. Saint-Roch was then the most important parish in the city and a bustling working-class district. Amanda and Arsène could read and write and signed their marriage record, as did their witnesses, which was exceptional for the time. The day before the wedding they signed a marriage contract before the notaire (notary) Louis Leclerc. At the Consignys'
The tragic death of Arsène Consigny. A mechanic-machinist, Arsène worked as foreman in the machine shop of his older brother Nicolas, on rue St-Joseph in the St-Roch district. The shop had a good reputation and was much in demand among the many boot and shoe factories of the St-Roch district. On March 14, 1893, the two brothers and a partner had obtained a federal patent, number CA 42294, for a "wire for making pegs", a piece of equipment that improved shoemaking machines.
