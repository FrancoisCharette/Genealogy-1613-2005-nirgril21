In Nicole's words

Marie-Anna Caron is born in Montréal on April 27, 1897. Her father, Arthur, is a tinsmith. He dies when she is eight years old. Her mother, Alphonsine Gagnon, then supports the family by taking in other people's laundry. In 1913 she remarries a widower, Zénon Laframboise. Marie-Anna receives a school education. Although her parents come from the Bas du Fleuve (the lower St. Lawrence), the family spends its whole life in the Saint-Henri district. Two parallel lives (Deux vies en parallèle)
Marie-Anna marries Rosario Charette on January 16, 1917, in St-Zotique parish in Montréal. It is a simple wedding, because Rosario has been in mourning for his father since the previous summer. His mother having also passed away, the couple settles into Rosario's parents' flat, furniture and all, at 1121 St-Antoine, St-Henri. Two years later they move to 444 St-Émélie, close to Marie-Anna's mother. Two parallel lives (Deux vies en parallèle)
Marie-Anna has seven children, one of them stillborn. She raises her family at 910 St-Philipppe, St-Henri, near the garage her husband had built, and runs, at 4271 St-Antoine. The family is comfortable; the children attend private schools. On weekends they visit relatives at their cottages, and they spend their holidays at the fashionable resorts of the day. They own a car, change it often, and travel, for example to Lac St-Jean to visit Marie-Anna's relatives. Two parallel lives (Deux vies en parallèle)
Marie-Anna loses her husband on May 21, 1940. He dies suddenly, though his health had been poor in the last part of his life. She has to rebuild her life, not least her finances. She rents a flat on rue St-Gérard and goes on raising her family there. Her eldest daughter is married from there the following year; her youngest is at boarding school. The boys start working or learn a trade. Two of her sons serve in the war in Europe. Two parallel lives (Deux vies en parallèle)
From 1946 to 1964 Marie-Anna has twenty-one grandchildren. In the 1950s she lives on the ground floor of a six-unit building that she bought on rue des Écores, in Montréal. Two of her sons and their families live in flats in the same building. Marie-Anna loves anything new and modern and buys it as soon as it comes on the market: a refrigerator, a shower, a TV... She always likes to have the latest model of everything, and her children buy it for her when they go to New York now and then. Two parallel lives (Deux vies en parallèle)
Marie-Anna dies on July 8, 1966, in Marieville. She had been living for some years in a nursing home run by nuns, needing constant care after a cancer of the intestines. Two parallel lives (Deux vies en parallèle)
All her life Marie-Anna has good friends; some even appear in the group photos at her children's weddings. Her friendship with one of them, Imelda Verreault, goes back to childhood. They were both born in 1897 and were neighbours, Marie-Anna living at 54 and Imelda at 63 rue Delinelle. 10th generation — Rosario and Marie-Anna — enfance
Marie-Anna has to seek advice and plan her finances. Her youngest is not yet five and her sons are still in school; as for herself, she has no work experience. She must therefore make sure that the inheritance she receives from her husband is managed so that it can provide for her needs for the rest of her life. In 1941 Marie-Anna moves to 7965 rue Saint-Gérard. She lives there for several years, marries off her children from there and goes on raising her youngest, Ghislaine, whom she sends to board at the convent in Saint-Aimé as soon as she reaches school age. Another war began in 1939, and two of her sons serve in it, Gérard in the regular army and Rosaire in the air force. Since housing is scarce at that time, some of her children live with her during the first part of their married lives. 10th generation — Rosario and Marie-Anna — m_a-ros
