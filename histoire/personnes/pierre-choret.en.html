In Nicole's words

Pierre Choret was born on February 24, 1690, in Beauport, and was baptized in the church of Notre-Dame de la Miséricorde; his godparents were Pierre Parent and Marie Chauveau. 3rd generation — Pierre — enfance
On February 1, 1712, Pierre Choret married Angélique Turcot in the parish of Saint-Charles-Borromée in Charlesbourg. 3rd generation — Pierre — vie
Around 1717 Pierre made a big decision: to leave the Quebec City region for the Montreal region. We don't know his reasons, but they were probably economic. Montreal had become the economic centre of Canada, and its rapid growth drew young people. This move was quite an expedition: more than 250 kilometres, no road fit for carts, young children and, surely, the household goods essential for setting the family up in its new home. Did they travel by river? Very probably; the St. Lawrence River was still, at the time, the main route, as it had been since the beginnings of the colony. 3rd generation — Pierre — vie
It seems that Pierre was a laboureur (farmer), like most people of the time. But people could also take on other jobs. We know that in 1727 Pierre Choret was "foreman of the ménagerie des pauvres" (poorhouse). That is, he was in charge of a kind of shelter and social assistance office. As early as the 17th century the government had set up "bureaux des pauvres" (poor relief offices) to help people in need. 3rd generation — Pierre — vie
Pierre saw his first grandchildren born before he died on July 22, 1749, in Sault-au-Récollet, at the age of 59. His widow Angélique remarried on October 5, 1750, to Jacques Messier, in Sault-au-Récollet. He died there on February 10, 1764. We do not yet know when Angélique died. 3rd generation — Pierre — vie
