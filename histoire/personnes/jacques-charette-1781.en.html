In Nicole's words

Jacques was born on April 2, 1781, at Sault-au-Récollet, like his father Jean-Baptiste and his grandfather Joseph. His great-grandfather Pierre had left the Québec City area to settle there at the beginning of the century. (N.B. he was baptized under the name Louis, but the records found to date strongly suggest that this is indeed Jacques, our ancestor.) 6th generation — Jacques — vie
On February 26, 1810, in Saint-Laurent de Montréal, Jacques married Marie-Amable-Marguerite Varin dite la Pistole. She was the daughter of Louis, a cooper, and Catherine Guilbault. The groom's witnesses were his brothers Jean-Baptiste and Louis Chauret. Jacques and Marie had signed a marriage contract on February 19 before notaire (notary) Barron. 6th generation — Jacques — vie
Jacques died in Montréal on August 5, 1869. His burial record says he was a day labourer, widower of Marie Varin, living in Notre-Dame parish and aged 98 1/2 (he was really closer to 88 1/2. The priest got his arithmetic wrong, or was misinformed. It is common, in official records, to find approximate or incorrect ages). Jacques had a long life and witnessed great political upheavals, including the Patriotes' Rebellion and Confederation... as well as incredible technical progress. 6th generation — Jacques — vie
So Jacques was, very probably, a militiaman. What were his political views? As today, most people probably wanted nothing more than a quiet life for their family. Putting bread on the table was not easy in those days, because harvests were often poor. Not to mention the epidemics of picote (smallpox) and scarlet fever (1783), smallpox (1784), cholera (1832-1854) and typhus (1847), which claimed thousands of victims. 6th generation — Jacques — vie
