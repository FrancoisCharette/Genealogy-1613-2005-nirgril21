In Nicole's words

Jacqueline, born August 7, 1926. Her godfather was Onésime Desjardins and her godmother his wife, Blanche Côté, the child's aunt. (Photo opposite. N.B. Francine still has this little child's chair) Jacqueline married Gérard Charette on June 29, 1946; her godfather was her witness. At the Consignys' — descendance
Ézilda's children started factory work at 15 or 16 to help their mother, paying her "board" every week. That was the way of life then: children had to help their parents financially as early in life as possible. Education beyond elementary school was not the norm. Jacqueline worked at American Can Co., at the corner of Ontario and Pie IX. Her uncle Antoine Consigny worked there and "got her hired", his young niece. Fabiola worked in a munitions factory during the war; she earned good wages there, but for risky work. At the Consignys' — VIE
Jacqueline set up a little scene on the back balcony of 2179 Létourneux. We suppose that she usually sat somewhere else to write to Gérard, but there is no doubt the engaged couple kept up a steady correspondence, given the number of postcards we still have from that time. At the Consignys' — VIE
All three of Ézilda's children married in 1946. For her two daughters, Fabiola and Jacqueline, she had to bear the whole cost of the wedding as "parent of the bride". Fabiola's wedding to Elphège Daoust was already planned for June 29, 1946, when Jacqueline decided to marry that same day as well, to Gérard Charette, recently back from Europe in the spring of 1946. So Ézilda's two daughters had a "double wedding". As for Arsène, he married Marthe Chaput on December 21 of the same year. At the Consignys' — VIE
On the morning of June 29, 1946, the bells ring out in Saint-Jean-Baptiste-de-Lasalle parish on rue Hochelaga, at the corner of Pie IX. Marie-Anna and Ézilda share one and the same event: the wedding of Gérard, Marie-Anna's son, and Jacqueline, Ézilda's daughter. They share the wedding meal at the newlyweds' head table. Had they met beforehand to get acquainted? No one knows anymore. The wedding was arranged quickly, Gérard having come back from Europe only a few weeks earlier. That same day Ézilda marries off both her daughters, and her son follows suit the next December. So Ézilda finds herself alone. As for Marie-Anna, she marries off her four sons between May 1945 and April 1948. She too ends up alone, her youngest, Ghislaine, being at boarding school. Even so, because of the housing shortage caused by the war, Marie-Anna and Ézilda each have to share their flat for a while with some of their newly married children.
