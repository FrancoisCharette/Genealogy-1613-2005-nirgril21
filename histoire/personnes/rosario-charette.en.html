In Nicole's words

Rosario is born on January 20, 1897, at 1096 rue Saint-Antoine, Saint-Henri. His parents are Alphonse Jr. and Marie Lavoie. His godparents are Alphonse Charette Sr. and Rosianne Miron, his paternal grandparents. Rosario is the third of nine children. All his sisters and brothers but one die in infancy. His father is a property owner and masonry contractor. At seventeen Rosario loses his mother and, two years later, his father. In 1924 his younger brother Alexandre dies as well. The family network, grandparents, uncles and aunts, cousins, surrounds Rosario, since they all live in Saint-Henri. 10th generation — Rosario and Marie-Anna — enfance
Marie-Anna and Rosario marry on January 16, 1917, in Saint-Zotique parish in Montréal. They are both under age; it is a simple wedding because Rosario is in mourning for his father. His guardian has to consent to the marriage. Marrying young is perfectly normal at the time, and young couples usually move in with one set of parents or the other. But Rosario and Marie-Anna are lucky enough to have their own home from the very start of their marriage: Marie-Anna simply joins Rosario in his parents' house, furniture and all, at 1121 rue Saint-Antoine. Alphonse Charette Jr. had built this house for his family in 1907. It is plausible that Alexandre, Rosario's fifteen-year-old brother, lived with the new couple; otherwise he was taken in by their guardian. 10th generation — Rosario and Marie-Anna — m_a-ros
Rosario received a good inheritance from his father but, since he is under age, his uncle and guardian, Aldéric Lavoie, manages his property. Aldéric is a carpenter, but he also has a business selling wood, coal and ice in Saint-Henri. Rosario works for him during the first years of his marriage. According to Fleurette Lavoie, Rosario's cousin, he and Marie-Anna "made a handsome couple". There is no doubt that Rosario loves his wife dearly, and he will treat her "like a queen" all his life. 10th generation — Rosario and Marie-Anna — m_a-ros
From 1917 to 1919 the young couple lives at 1121 Saint-Antoine; Rosario is listed as ice mcht. From 1919 to 1923 they live at 444 Sainte-Émélie, diagonally across from Marie-Anna's mother, Alphonsine, who lives at 455 Sainte-Émélie. Rosario's occupation is listed as mech. From 1924 to 1926 they live at 284 Saint-Philippe. From 1926 to 1928 they are at 266 Saint-Philippe. Rosario is listed as wood & coal 1117 St-Antoine. 1117 St-Antoine is right at the corner of Saint-Philippe, on the site of the garage Rosario will later have built. 10th generation — Rosario and Marie-Anna — m_a-ros
In1926 Rosario's family moves to 266 Saint-Philippe, which will become 910 when the street numbers change in 1928. They live on the ground floor of this three-storey building for more than ten years. Rosario rents out the upper floors. It is very close to the corner of Saint-Antoine, where Rosario works. He is said to have owned several other properties inherited from his father and his brother. The family often goes to the country to visit relatives and friends who have cottages. They also rent cottages themselves for the holidays. The family also travels to the Saguenay region to visit Marie-Anna's relatives. One of them had multiple sclerosis, and Rosario is said to have built a kind of wheeled cart so he could get around (Story passed on by Ghislaine, who heard it from her family). Rosario loves cars and changes them often. He gets that from his father and his grandfather Charette. 10th generation — Rosario and Marie-Anna — m_a-ros
In November 1928 Rosario files at Montréal city hall the plans for a garage he wants to build at 4271 Saint-Antoine. Note that the same year the street numbering changes and1117 Saint-Antoine becomes 4271. In 1930, the Garage Philippe appears for the first time at 4271 Saint-Antoine, corner of Saint-Philippe, in the Lovell directory. Rosario is clearly identified as the owner, and remains so until 1938. 10th generation — Rosario and Marie-Anna — m_a-ros
THE "GARAGE PHILIPPE", A MODERN GARAGE The garage's gas pumps are open 24 hours a day, 7 days a week. The garage has four floors; cars are taken up to the floors by an elevator. That is where Rosario's young sons learn to drive. Rosario's employees have fun letting the boss's sons drive the cars in and out of the elevator (Gérard's recollection). Rosario's accountant is Henri Philippe, who is also one of his tenants. His wife, Imelda Verreault, is a good friend of Marie-Anna's. Their daughter Rachel will marry Rosaire, Rosario's son. At 4277 Saint-Antoine (part of the garage's ground floor) Rosario rents a shop from the start, and until 1939, to Napoléon Lapierre, who sets up a shoe-repair shop there. 10th generation — Rosario and Marie-Anna — m_a-ros
In the late 1930s Rosario is ill; his son Jean-Noël remembers a home nurse putting cupping glasses on his father's back as he leaned over the living-room piano. What illness does he have? Ghislaine remembers always hearing that her father had a "coeur de boeuf" (an ox heart), that is, a heart problem that kept him from heavy physical exertion. Rosario rents out his whole garage for $100 a month, no longer able to run his business. Shortly afterwards, his tenant sublets each floor for $500 a month to the Canadian army! Rosario "takes it personally" (Jean-Noël's recollection), that is, he sees it as proof of his incompetence in business. This financial loss, on top of others, undermines the morale of the discouraged Rosario and deals him a fatal blow. 10th generation — Rosario and Marie-Anna — m_a-ros
From the Lovell directory* of 1939-1940 we learn that Rosario has sold 910 Saint-Philippe to "Ménard Vic et Daoust Miss Cecile" and that he has moved to 339 Saint-Philippe. As for his business, we can assume he sold it too, since 4271 Saint-Antoine has become the Garage Bessette 10th generation — Rosario and Marie-Anna — m_a-ros
Rosario dies on May 21, 1940, at the age of 42. His cousin Yolande Lavoie, who has just finished her nursing course, is at his bedside the night he dies. She had looked after Rosario in his last days, and also Marie-Anna, sorely tried by her husband's illness, and the youngest children. (Story passed on by Ghislaine, who heard it from Rita and Fleurette Lavoie, Rosario's cousins) 10th generation — Rosario and Marie-Anna — m_a-ros
