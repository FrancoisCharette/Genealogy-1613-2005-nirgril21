Mathieu Choret is one of the pioneers who put down roots in New France. After a century of failed attempts, the settlement of Canada truly begins in 1634. Mathieu settles there in 1647 with his wife, Sébastienne Veillon.

 Since we know only the broad outlines of Mathieu's and Sébastienne's lives, we can get a better picture of them by placing them in their historical context. So, to understand their times, you will find a few keys below.   

 

           Westward to Asia... 

A turning point in Western history: on May 29, 1453,  Constantinople falls to the Ottoman Turks. This marks the end of the long historical period known as the Middle Ages. The last Byzantine scholars and artists take refuge in Italy, where they will give rise to the Renaissance. In France, the Hundred Years' War, the last feudal-style conflict, comes to an end.    read more>>>

Earlier historians, staunchly Catholic, glossed over the fact that Québec City was founded by a Huguenot, Samuel de Champlain. Indeed, today's historians think it quite likely that Champlain was a Protestant; his patron certainly was.  read more>>>
