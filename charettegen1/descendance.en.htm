Mathieu Choret and Sébastienne Veillon had seven children, six sons, including twins, and one daughter. Rare for the time, all their children lived to adulthood.  

Mathieu and Sébastienne went on to have nearly seventy-five grandchildren.  

That is what you might call a large posterity.

Here are Mathieu and Sébastienne's children:

Robert, born on June 7, 1648, in Quebec, is baptized at home by his father (ondoiement, an emergency baptism).  At his baptism his godparents are Robert Hache, his father's friend and fellow worker with the Jesuits, and Barbara Reboux.

Robert marries Madeleine Paradis, born on January 12, 1655, in Quebec, on April 22, 1674, in Quebec.  They have six children when Madeleine dies on September 2, 1684, in Quebec. These children would marry into the Le Normand, Rainville, Marcoux, Duquet and Lemire families.

In the 1681 census Robert and his family are listed as living at St-Laurent, Île d'Orléans.  He is a carpenter by trade and lives on land near that of his father-in-law, Pierre Paradis.  He has a gun, seven head of cattle and fifteen arpents of cleared land.

There are several notarized contracts in which Robert Choret hires himself out as a joiner, for instance on the building of the cathedral of Quebec, or lends money or buys land. In 1700, for example, he buys the farm of his niece Françoise (daughter of Joseph Choret) at St-Pierre, Île d'Orléans.  His name also appears in judgments of the Sovereign Council.

Robert remarries on February 7, 1686, in Quebec, to Marguerite Le Rouge.  They have eleven children, who would marry into the Martin, Massé, Lemay, Auger, Grenier, Lemire, Biron and Petit families.  Several would live in the Lotbinière area.  

Indeed, Robert moved to that area, more precisely to Ste-Croix, at the beginning of the 18th century.  His last three children were born there between 1700 and 1705. 

On April 5, 1701, Robert buys the seigneury of Pointe-aux-Bouleaux, near Ste-Croix de Lotbinière, from the widow of Mathieu Amiot.  He has to give up a quarter of it to Charles Amiot by a judgment of the Prévôté of Quebec on May 14, 1708, and sells the rest to Pierre-Noël Le Gardeur on August 21, 1714.

Robert dies on June 15, 1716, at Ste-Croix, at the age of 68. 

Note that Jean Charest,  Premier of Quebec, is a descendant of Robert Choret and his second wife.

Joseph, born on August 14, 1650, in Beauport, is baptized at home by Robert Giffard.  At his baptism on September 8, 1650, in Beauport, his godparents are Robert Giffard, Seigneur of Beauport, and Marie-Françoise de Clément de Vrault.

Joseph is confirmed on May 5, 1662, by Bishop de Laval.

He marries Anne Loignon, born in 1653, on October 29, 1676, at Ste-Famille, Île d'Orléans.

In the 1681 census Joseph and his family are listed as living at St-Laurent, Île d'Orléans.  He owns a gun, four head of cattle and fifteen arpents of cleared land.

Joseph has three children; they would live at St-Pierre, Île d'Orléans, and two of them would marry into the Costé and Trudel families.  The third would become a nun at the Hôpital Général of Quebec.

Joseph dies on October 28, 1684, at the Hôtel-Dieu of Quebec, in the paupers' ward, and is buried in the paupers' cemetery of that hospital.  He is 35.

Joseph's widow, Anne Loignon, marries Antoine Paulet as her second husband on February 13, 1685.  She dies on November 25, 1722, at St-Pierre. 

In 1700, part of the land that had belonged to Joseph, at St-Laurent, Île d'Orléans, was used to build that parish's church.

 

Jeanne, born on December 11, 1652, is baptized at home right away and then baptized on January 12, 1653, in Quebec by Father Ragueneau, superior of the Jesuits, who records the act in Quebec.  Her godparents are Robert Drouin and Jeanne Brant.

On January 14, 1669, Jeanne marries Jean Moricet, born on August 18, 1641, at Saint-Gilles-de-Surgères, a market town about 30 km east of La Rochelle.  The wedding takes place in the Beauport chapel, officiated by Thomas Morel, a Jesuit missionary priest of the Séminaire de Québec.  The witnesses are Paul Vachon, the Choret family's notaire, and two habitants of Beauport.

In the 1681 census the couple has thirty arpents of cleared land.  

They have fourteen children, all born at Ste-Famille, Île d'Orléans.

Jean Moricet dies on August 15, 1699, at Ste-Famille.  Jeanne does not remarry; she seems comfortable financially, and her children can support her.

Jeanne dies on September 26, 1718, at the age of 66.

Jeanne's children would marry into the Asselin, Létourneau, Clément, Catrin and Simon families.  Three of them would marry Cloutiers, and one of the daughters would marry a Loignon, Charles Loignon, brother of her aunt Jeanne, wife of Joseph Choret.

Note that Claude Comtois, Francine Charette's partner, is also descended from Mathieu Choret, but through his daughter Jeanne.  

Pierre the elder, one of the twins, is born on January 17, 1655, in Quebec.  He is baptized in March 1655; no godparent is mentioned in the birth certificate.  

Pierre marries Madeleine Giroux on January 21, 1686, in the Beauport chapel, after a contract drawn up by the notaire Paul Vachon.  It is a double wedding, since his brother Pierre the Younger marries the same day, in the same place.

Pierre has more than ten children, but several die in infancy.  A child born in 1696 has as godfather Pierre Vachon, the son of the family notaire.  In 1699, the youngest has as godparents the Sieur Ignace Juchereau du Chesnaye and his sister Louise, wife of the captain of the colonial troops, Alexandre de l'Estringan.  This child would marry in Charlesbourg and die in Montréal in 1764.  Ignace Juchereau du Chesnaye is the grandson and heir of Robert Giffard, Seigneur of Beauport.  Through his wife he also inherits the seigneury of Fossambault.

Pierre's children would marry into the Imbeault, Cliche, Jobin, Rotureau and Sarrasin families.

Pierre dies on July 27, 1701, at the Hôtel-Dieu of Quebec, at the age of 46. 

His widow remarries on January 10, 1705, in Charlesbourg, to Maurice Rhéaume.

 

Ignace, born on January 17, 1655, is baptized on August 29, 1655, in Quebec, several months after his twin brother.   Why?  Perhaps the twins were separated after birth, since their mother could not nurse them both.  Ignace may have been put out to a wet nurse and baptized when he came back to the family.  It seems a plausible hypothesis to me.

Ignace marries Marie Bélanger in Beauport on November 26, 1685.  He is a shoemaker and farmer in Beauport.

Ignace has five children, all born in Beauport.  They would marry into the Ferré, Petit, Laurent, Beaugis and Picher families.  The youngest, born in 1698, would marry a Beaugis, a relative of his aunt, the wife of Pierre the Younger. 

Ignace dies at 46, on October 6, 1701, at the Hôtel-Dieu of Quebec, a few months after his twin brother Pierre the elder.  Mathieu and Sébastienne's two twins die in the same year, less than three months apart.

His widow remarries on November 9, 1705, in Beauport, to Jacques Parent.

 

Jean, born on November 27, 1657, is baptized in Quebec on December 9, 1657.  His godparents are Jean Le Vasseur and Marie Lelart.

Jean marries Claire Baucher on October 30, 1684, at Ste-Famille, Île d'Orléans.  In the marriage deed drawn up before the notaire Paul Vachon, Jean says he lives at St-Pierre, Île d'Orléans.

His eight children would marry into the Ouellet, Boissonneau, Lepage, Liénard, Lis, Roussin and Ricosse families.  Some would live in Neuville, others in St-Vallier in Bellechasse.

Jean dies on June 6, 1699, still at St-Pierre, Île d'Orléans; he is 42..

 

Pierre the Younger, born on January 21, 1662, is baptized in the Beauport chapel.

He marries there, at the same time as his brother Pierre the elder,  on January 21, 1686, Marie-Anne Beaugis, born on January 27, 1669, in Quebec.  They had signed a marriage contract before the notaire Paul Vachon.  Their witnesses at the wedding are the notaire and Robert Choret, Pierre's eldest brother.  The bride and groom cannot sign their names.  Note that Robert himself would remarry a few days later, on February 7.  

Pierre the Younger has eighteen children.  They are all born in Beauport, but several would move to the Montréal area, where they would marry.  They would marry into the Turcot, Brault, Chrétien, Roy, Huppé, Magnan , Maurier, Martin, Gauthier, Rapidiou, Larouche, Bouthillet, Trottier, Maheu and Taillon families.

Pierre the Younger dies on May 5, 1736, in Beauport, at the age of  74.  With him dies the last member of the first generation of Chorets born in New France.  Nearly ninety years had passed since his parents had settled there in 1647. 

We are descended from Pierre the Younger.
