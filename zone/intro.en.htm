It is not for us to judge those who came before us. We work from what they did, mistakes included. And those who come after us will do the same.

Québec has a beautiful history.

You doubt it? Your school memories are of colons and habitants (settlers and farmers, two words that have become pejorative), and you feel like "losers" when you think of the Conquest, the Patriotes, the referendums...?  

And yet, when you look at Québec's history from the other end of the telescope, with more distance, you find it beautiful. It often takes someone from outside to tell us before we realize it. Within five years I took two different training courses on the experience of immigrants and how they see Quebecers. Both trainers were immigrant women who had lived in Québec for many years and who, to better understand their adopted country, had studied our history (five years ago it was a Cuban woman and, recently, a Mexican woman). 

What they said was the same: "You have a beautiful history, a moving one, a story of the tenacity and survival of a young people. Rooted in French,  and therefore European and Latin, soil, and  later coloured by British and American influences, your history has made you something unique. Studying it made me love Québec and Quebecers even more."

Let us forget the diehard Catholic and nationalist propaganda of our old history books.  Let us look at the facts with good plain common sense. The first ancestors, those of the 17th century,  showed admirable will and courage in choosing to settle in a country to which their way of life was not at all suited. But very quickly  their ways and their outlook changed. They became different from the French who came here only to advance their careers. The English Conquest of 1759 is a good thing for the Canadiens, the French colony being on the brink of ruin at the time. Of course, the  English would have liked to assimilate them, but, not being numerous enough and wanting the Canadiens on their side rather than on the side of the Americans, then in the midst of their revolution for independence, the English conquerors negotiated with the Canadiens,  thereby allowing them to survive as a distinct people.   

Ever since, the Canadiens, then the French Canadians, then the Quebecers, and now the French-speaking Quebecers have been marked by their knack for negotiating and making compromises in order to survive and grow. Québec's reality is changing quickly;  the story continues....
