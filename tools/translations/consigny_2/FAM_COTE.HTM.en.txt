A memory of Claude Côté, Émilien's son</u>: &quot;I know there was no
      great love between Évangéline and her mother Georgiana. Georgiana
      put Évangéline out of the house with her suitcase on the
      doorstep. Her nickname was <i>Ti-Cul </i>(she was very small).
      Évangéline always lived with a common-law partner, Philémon
      Chiasson, known as the rag man. He had a gold tooth and was rather
      shabby and not too clean. They lived in a scrapyard whose
      entrance was at the corner of De LaSalle and Lafontaine streets.&nbsp; Their
      flat was at the back of the yard, on the second floor; it was
      very small and very clean.&quot;

Two years later

Ézilda
        told me herself that she wanted to become a nun. So she
        entered the convent, probably in Montreal, since that is where she
        lived from 1903 on.<span style="mso-spacerun: yes">
        </span>Some time later, she<span style="mso-spacerun: yes"> </span>fell
ill and the religious community's superiors sent her back to her family
        because,<span style="mso-spacerun: yes"> </span>judged to be in fragile health
and illiterate, she was of no use to the community.<o:p>
        Ézilda took a job in a shoe factory in the neighbourhood and
        worked there until her marriage.&nbsp;<br>
        <br>
        </span></font><span style="mso-bidi-font-size: 12.0pt"><font size="3"> We
        do not know how Charles and Ézilda met, or when they
        got engaged. The only clue we have is that <span style="mso-bidi-font-size: 12.0pt; mso-fareast-font-family: Times New Roman; mso-ansi-language: FR-CA; mso-fareast-language: FR; mso-bidi-language: AR-SA"> the husband of
        Crescence Consigny, Charles's sister, was Rolland Pleau, a cousin of Ézilda's
        on her mother's side. We can suppose that this family tie led to
        Charles and Ézilda meeting. In all likelihood</span>,
Charles already knew Ézilda before leaving for Europe in 1914, since he wrote
to her during the war and married her as soon as he got back in 1919.<span style="mso-spacerun: yes">&nbsp;In those days,
        soldiers had&nbsp; no leave during their service, so Charles must
        have known Ézilda before going to war.
