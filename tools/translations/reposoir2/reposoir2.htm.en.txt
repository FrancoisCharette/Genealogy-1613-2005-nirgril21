@@ title
For centuries
@@ 1
For
centuries, on certain occasions,&nbsp; religious ceremonies were held
outdoors, in front of a reposoir (a temporary wayside altar).&nbsp; It all began with
a procession from the church to the spot chosen for the reposoir.&nbsp;
For the Fête-Dieu (Corpus Christi) in June, for instance, the parish priest, carrying the monstrance and surrounded by
his clergy and the altar boys, led the procession, followed by the local notables
and the
townspeople.&nbsp; Members of a parish organization walked
together, for example the Enfants de Marie (Children of Mary, the young girls), the
members of the J.O.C. (Jeunesse ouvrière catholique, Young Catholic Workers),&nbsp; of the J.E.C.
(Jeunesse étudiante catholique, Young Catholic Students), the Dames de Ste-Anne (Ladies of St. Anne, the older women),
the Knights of Columbus, the League of the Sacred Heart, the Scouts and the Guides,
etc...&nbsp; Zouaves (papal soldiers) might also accompany the procession.&nbsp;
Each group carried its banner and wore its costume.&nbsp; The place a person or a group held
in the procession
mattered, and could stir up rivalries.

Fête-Dieu (Corpus Christi) procession in St-Zotique parish, Montréal, in

@@ 3
Everyone
gathered in front of the reposoir, and the priest then performed the ceremony.&nbsp;
The reposoir was&nbsp; an altar built in front of a private home.&nbsp;
A whole set was built around the altar, in which&nbsp; children in costume were
placed as extras.&nbsp;&nbsp; Each year one home in the parish was chosen
for the reposoir, which was a great honour for the
owner of the house.
