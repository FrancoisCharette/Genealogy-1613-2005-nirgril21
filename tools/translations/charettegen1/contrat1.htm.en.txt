@@ frtitle
Contrat de concession d'une terre
@@ title
Land grant contract
@@ 1
Contract for the
        grant of land by the Jesuit Fathers to Mathieu Choret, drawn up
        before the notaire (notary) Claude Berman in Quebec on March 6, 1649.
@@ 2
(full text
        as copied in the book by the genealogist Brother Jean-Benoit
        Charette; rendered here in English)
@@ 3
&quot; On the sixth day of March,
        sixteen hundred and forty-nine, there appeared in person the Reverend Father
        Jérôme Lalemant, Superior of the missions of the Society of Jesus in
        New France, residing in their house in Quebec, who, acting
        for the said Society and wishing to see the uninhabited lands
        of their grant put to use, has voluntarily given and granted, by title of cens
        et rentes seigneuriales (seigneurial dues), payable each year on the feast day of Saint
        Stephen, the day after Christmas, to Mathieu Chourel, residing in the said Quebec,
        here present and accepting for himself, his heirs and assigns in future,
        a parcel of three arpents of land frontage on the Great River of
        Saint Lawrence in New France and four leagues deep
        into the woods, the said three arpents of frontage beginning next to the
        Bonsecours farm belonging to the Reverend Jesuit Fathers on the side of
        Monsieur Giffard, and on the other side the grant of Michel Huppé
        dit la Croix; at one end along the great Saint Lawrence River, and at
        the other end the lands of the Gentlemen of the Company not yet granted;
        all of which grants are laid out on lines or rhumbs of
        wind running from south-east to north-west or thereabouts, which is the length, the
        said grants being four leagues deep into the woods, as
        aforesaid, and three arpents wide throughout.&nbsp; The Reverend Fathers
        intend to reserve a road across all the grants
        belonging to them from Notre-Dame-des-Anges to Beauport; the
        lines or roads bounding the said grant shall serve as roads, for
        the said Mathieu Chauret, his heirs or assigns, to enjoy forever.
        The present grant is made on the following charges and conditions, namely
        that the said Mathieu Chouret has promised and bound himself to pay
        each year on the said feast day of Saint Stephen, for each arpent of
        frontage on the great Saint Lawrence River, the sum of thirty
        sols of ground rent under a perpetual, non-redeemable lease, with one
        double of cens for the whole of the said grant, and two live capons, also as
        rent, each year on the said day of Saint Stephen; and in case the said
        Chouret, his heirs and assigns have no capons, they shall pay
        thirty sols for the said two capons, without fraud, at our
        seigneurial manor in Quebec, beginning to pay the said cens and
        rents on the said day of Saint Stephen of the year sixteen hundred and forty-
        nine; the said cens and rents to the Reverend Fathers carrying lods et ventes (transfer dues),
        seisin and fines according to the custom of the city, provostship and viscounty of
        Paris as received in the said country of New France; and further on condition that
        the said party shall have land cleared and build on the said place within one year
        from this day at the latest, and in the following years cultivate the said land
        so that the said cens and rents can be paid each
        year; failing which the said Reverend Fathers, as lessors, are permitted to
        take back possession of the said lands as abandoned by him, by full
        right, without any form or semblance of legal process. The said Mathieu Chouret,
        his heirs and assigns shall be bound to have their grain ground at the mill of the said
        Reverend Fathers, should one be built and set up, on the lands of
        Notre-Dame-des-Anges or on those of Sainte-Anne, called La Vacherie,
        belonging to the said Reverend Fathers, and also provided it is in a state
        to grind. For so they promise and bind themselves, each in his own right. Made and
        executed in the presence of the honourable Jean Juchereau, sieur de Maur, and
        Nicolas Marsolet, witnesses, who have signed... The said Chouret declared
        that he could neither write nor sign and made his usual mark.&quot;
