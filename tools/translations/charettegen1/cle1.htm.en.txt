The end of the Middle Ages
@@ 1
The end of the
      Middle Ages
@@ 2
A turning point in Western history: <b><font color="#742D29">May
      29, 1453,&nbsp; the fall of Constantinople</font></b> to the
      Ottoman Turks.&nbsp; It marks the end of the long historical period called
      the Middle Ages.&nbsp; The last Byzantine scholars and artists take refuge
      in Italy, where they will give rise to the Renaissance.&nbsp; In France,
      the Hundred Years' War, the last feudal-style conflict, is drawing to a close.&nbsp;&nbsp;
      With the Turkish victory, the West is cut off from its Greek and
      Eastern roots.&nbsp; Its trade links with&nbsp; China and the
      Indies, which enjoyed Byzantine protection, are
      broken.&nbsp; Yet this Silk Road trade is essential to Europe; another
      route must be found to keep obtaining spices,
      dyes, perfumes, precious stones,&nbsp; coral,&nbsp; and exotic
      fabrics such as silk…
@@ 3
At that time Europeans
      know of three continents
@@ 4
So they look for a sea route that
      will take them back to the East so they can carry on their trade.&nbsp;&nbsp; </font>Daring
      Portuguese sailors make several voyages around Africa, and&nbsp;
      <font color="#742D29"><b>Vasco da Gama</b>  </font> is the first to reach the Indies, in 1498.&nbsp; But this
      route is long, costly and dangerous.
@@ 5
Christopher Columbus</font> </b>(1451-1506)
      looks west instead.&nbsp;&nbsp; It had been known since Antiquity that
      the earth is round; by cutting across the Atlantic he thinks he can quickly
      reach Asia, the Cipangu (Japan) Marco
      Polo wrote about.&nbsp;&nbsp; But that takes money; he offers his plan to
      several kings, who turn him down.
@@ 6
After years of effort he finds the
      funding he needs from the sovereigns of Spain, <b><font color="#742D29">Ferdinand
      of Aragon and Isabella of Castile</font></b>.&nbsp;&nbsp; To the king he promises
      gold; to the queen, souls.&nbsp;&nbsp; The queen is indeed very
      devout, and she is led to believe that Islam, whose empire has been spreading&nbsp;
      since the fall of Constantinople,&nbsp; could be taken from behind through
      Asia (with Catholic priests converting the peoples of Asia before
      the Muslims could convert them).&nbsp; All this is nothing but
      illusion.&nbsp; But an empire will come of it in the end.&nbsp; And it is
      indeed gold and silver by the ton that will pour into the ports of
      Castile and flow through the economy of Europe.
@@ 7
After two months at sea Christopher
      Columbus makes landfall on October 12, 1492, convinced he has reached the edge
      of the Asian continent.&nbsp; He will make four voyages and die with that
      conviction.
@@ 8
Columbus is the
      official discoverer of America, but several others had come before
      him.&nbsp; They appear in the legends of the Middle Ages, in
      Irish folklore, and in the archaeological traces left by the
      Vikings and the Basques.
@@ 9
The race to the
      West is on...
@@ 10
As arbiter of the Christian world, <b><font color="#742D29">Pope Alexander VI</font></b>
      steps in as early as 1493.&nbsp; He draws an arbitrary line from the North Pole to the South Pole,
      passing through the Azores:&nbsp; everything to the east goes to the Portuguese
      and, to the west, to the Spanish. !!!&nbsp; Naturally this does not
      suit the other nations, who want their share of the spoils.&nbsp;
      England reacts quickly, and Henry VII sponsors a voyage of
      discovery, which he too entrusts to an Italian, <b><font color="#742D29">Giovanni Caboto</font></b> (John Cabot).&nbsp;
      On June 24, 1497, Cabot lands on a shore in the North
      Atlantic (probably Cape Breton).&nbsp; He too believes he has
      reached Asia.
@@ 11
There are riches to be
      exploited there, at least in the fisheries.&nbsp; This
      &quot;new found land&quot; is explored by the English, but also by the Portuguese,
      who, though they had been given the eastern part of the world, send a voyage of
      discovery in 1500, convinced that this &quot;new found land&quot; lies
      east of the papal line of demarcation.&nbsp; What remains of these Portuguese
      voyages is the first ethnographic report Europe received on
      the Indigenous peoples of North America, written by <b> <font color="#742D29"> Miguel Real

In 1513 the Spanish explorer
      <b><font color="#742D29">Balboa</font></b> crosses the narrow strip of Panama and discovers another
      ocean.&nbsp;… A mystery to everyone: the globe is bigger
      than people had imagined.&nbsp; Between the Atlantic and Asia, a
      second ocean must be added, its width as unknown as that of the new
      lands just discovered.&nbsp; Of these lands, people distinguish
      America (what we now call South America) and the
      Antilles.&nbsp; But in the north, what does the world look like?&nbsp; That
      same year <b><font color="#742D29">Ponce de León</font> </b>reaches Florida.
