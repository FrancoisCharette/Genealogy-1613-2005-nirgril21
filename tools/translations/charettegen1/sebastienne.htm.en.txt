The son of Pierre Aufroy and Hilaire Milsandre, <b> Pierre</b> was born

WHY THIS MARRIAGE?
@@ 25
There was an age difference of nearly
                twenty years between Sébastienne and
                Pierre.&nbsp; One may wonder what each of them
                gained from this marriage and whether they really lived together.
@@ 26
For Sébastienne</b>, she had to ensure the
                survival of her family.&nbsp; And for that, a head of the family was
                essential.&nbsp; <font face="Times New Roman">At that
                time, a woman was considered a minor all her
                life, first under her father's guardianship, then her
                husband's.&nbsp; It was not <b> socially</b> acceptable for
                Sébastienne to stay alone on her land with her young children, and
                still less for her to take on a hired man, which would have given her
                a bad reputation.&nbsp; There had to be, officially at
                least, a head of the family.&nbsp; In any case, staying alone
                was <b>
                economically</b> impossible, as farm work was too
                hard.
@@ 27
We have already seen that
                romance had no place in the idea of marriage at
                that time, and that the couple, in a colony being settled,
                was the basis of the family enterprise.&nbsp; Widowers and widows
                with young children all remarried quickly;
                &quot;blended families&quot; were common, and the
                second union often produced more children.&nbsp; Only
                older widowers and widows could remain
                single.&nbsp; So Sébastienne's only daughter, Jeanne, would not
                remarry after her husband's death, since she could count on her grown children to work the land
                and look after her.
@@ 28
For Pierre</b>, this marriage
                freed him from his indenture and let him settle in the
                country.&nbsp; Sébastienne had inherited half of
                Mathieu's property, which was no small thing.&nbsp; In 1664 there were
                very few marriageable women in the colony; the population
                of about 3,000 people scattered along the St. Lawrence was
                mostly men.&nbsp;&nbsp;The gradual arrival of the
                &quot;Filles du Roy&quot; (King's Daughters) had begun the year before, but
                they were not numerous and chose men who were already
                established, which Pierre Aufroy was not.
