@@ title
Descendants of Mathieu Choret
@@ 1
Mathieu
        Choret and Sébastienne Veillon had seven children, six sons, including
        twins, and one daughter. Rare for the time, all their children
        lived to adulthood.
@@ 2
Mathieu
        and Sébastienne went on to have nearly
        seventy-five grandchildren.
@@ 3
That is what
        you might call a large posterity.
@@ 4
Here are
        Mathieu and Sébastienne's children:
@@ 5
Robert</b></font>, born on June 7, 1648, in Quebec, is
        baptized at home by his father (ondoiement, an emergency baptism).&nbsp; At his baptism his godparents are Robert
        Hache, his father's friend and fellow worker with the Jesuits, and
        Barbara Reboux.
@@ 6
Robert marries <b>Madeleine Paradis</b>, born on January 12, 1655, in
        Quebec, on April 22, 1674, in
        Quebec.&nbsp; They have six children when Madeleine dies on September
        2, 1684, in Quebec. These children would marry into the Le Normand,
        Rainville, Marcoux, Duquet and Lemire families.
@@ 7
In the 1681 census Robert and his
        family are listed as living at St-Laurent, Île d'Orléans.&nbsp;
        He is a carpenter by trade and lives on land near that
        of his father-in-law, Pierre Paradis.&nbsp; He has a gun, seven head of
        cattle and fifteen arpents of cleared land.
@@ 8
There are several notarized contracts
        in which Robert Choret hires himself out as a joiner, for instance on the building of the
        cathedral of Quebec, or lends money or buys
        land. In 1700, for example, he buys the farm of his niece
        Françoise (daughter of Joseph Choret) at St-Pierre, Île d'Orléans.&nbsp;
        His name also appears in judgments of the Sovereign Council.
@@ 9
Robert remarries on February 7, 1686, in
        Quebec, to <b>Marguerite Le Rouge</b>.&nbsp; They have eleven children, who
        would marry into the Martin, Massé, Lemay, Auger, Grenier,
        Lemire, Biron and Petit families.&nbsp; Several would live in the
        Lotbinière area.
@@ 10
Indeed, Robert moved to that
        area, more precisely to Ste-Croix, at the beginning of the 18th
        century.&nbsp; His last three children were born there between 1700 and
        1705.
@@ 11
On April 5, 1701, Robert buys the <b>seigneury of Pointe-aux-Bouleaux</b>, near
        Ste-Croix de Lotbinière, from the
        widow of Mathieu Amiot.&nbsp; He has to give up a quarter of it to Charles
        Amiot by a judgment of the Prévôté of Quebec on May 14, 1708, and sells the
        rest to Pierre-Noël Le Gardeur on August 21, 1714.
@@ 12
Robert dies on June 15, 1716, at
        Ste-Croix, at the age of 68.
@@ 13
Note
      that <b>Jean Charest</b>,&nbsp; Premier of
      Quebec, is a descendant of Robert Choret and his second wife.

link
      to his family tree
@@ 15
Joseph</b></font>, born on August 14, 1650, in Beauport, is baptized at home by Robert
        Giffard.&nbsp; At his baptism on September 8, 1650, in Beauport, his godparents
        are Robert Giffard, Seigneur of Beauport, and Marie-Françoise de
        Clément de Vrault.
@@ 16
Joseph is confirmed on May 5, 1662, by Bishop de Laval.
@@ 17
He marries <b>Anne
        Loignon</b>, born in 1653, on October 29, 1676, at Ste-Famille, Île d'Orléans.
@@ 18
In the 1681 census Joseph and his family are listed as
        living at St-Laurent, Île d'Orléans.&nbsp; He owns a gun, four
        head of cattle and fifteen arpents of cleared land.
@@ 19
Joseph has three children; they would live at St-Pierre, Île d'Orléans,
        and two of them would marry into the Costé and Trudel families.&nbsp; The
        third would become a nun at the Hôpital Général of Quebec.
@@ 20
Joseph dies on October 28, 1684, at the Hôtel-Dieu of Quebec, in
        the paupers' ward, and is buried in the paupers' cemetery of that
        hospital.&nbsp; He is 35.
@@ 21
Joseph's widow, Anne Loignon, marries Antoine
        Paulet as her second husband on February 13, 1685.&nbsp; She dies on November 25, 1722, at
        St-Pierre.
@@ 22
In 1700, part of the land that had belonged
        to Joseph, at
        St-Laurent, Île d'Orléans, was used to build that parish's church.
@@ 23
Jeanne</b></font>, born on December 11, 1652, is
        baptized at home right away and then baptized on January 12, 1653, in
        Quebec by Father Ragueneau, superior of the Jesuits, who records
        the act in Quebec.&nbsp; Her
        godparents are Robert Drouin and Jeanne Brant.
@@ 24
On January 14, 1669, Jeanne marries <b>Jean Moricet</b>, born
        on August 18, 1641, at Saint-Gilles-de-Surgères, a market town about
        30 km east of La Rochelle.&nbsp; The wedding
        takes place in the Beauport chapel, officiated by Thomas Morel,
        a Jesuit missionary priest of the Séminaire de Québec.&nbsp; The
        witnesses are Paul Vachon, the Choret family's notaire, and two
        habitants of Beauport.
@@ 25
In the 1681 census the couple has thirty arpents of cleared
        land.
@@ 26
They have fourteen children, all born at Ste-Famille, Île d'Orléans.
@@ 27
Jean Moricet dies on August 15, 1699, at Ste-Famille.&nbsp; Jeanne
        does not remarry; she seems comfortable financially, and her children
        can support her.
@@ 28
Jeanne dies on September 26, 1718, at the age of 66.
@@ 29
Jeanne's children would marry into the Asselin, Létourneau, Clément, Catrin and Simon families.&nbsp; Three of them would marry
        Cloutiers, and one of the daughters would marry a Loignon, Charles Loignon,
        brother of her aunt Jeanne, wife of Joseph Choret.
@@ 30
Note that<b> Claude Comtois</b>,
        Francine Charette's partner, is also descended from Mathieu Choret, but through
        his daughter Jeanne.
@@ 31
Pierre the elder, </b></font>one of the
        twins, is born
        on January 17, 1655, in Quebec.&nbsp; He is baptized in March 1655; no
        godparent is mentioned in the birth certificate.
@@ 32
Pierre marries <b>Madeleine
        Giroux</b> on January 21, 1686, in the Beauport chapel, after a contract drawn up by the notaire Paul
        Vachon.&nbsp; It is a double wedding, since his brother Pierre the Younger marries the same day, in the same
        place.
@@ 33
Pierre has more than ten children, but several die in
        infancy.&nbsp; A child born in 1696 has as godfather Pierre Vachon, the son
        of the family notaire.&nbsp; In 1699, the youngest has as
        godparents the Sieur Ignace Juchereau du Chesnaye and his sister Louise, wife of the captain of the
        colonial troops, Alexandre de l'Estringan.&nbsp; This child would marry
        in Charlesbourg and die in Montréal in 1764.&nbsp; <i>Ignace
        Juchereau du Chesnaye is the grandson and heir of Robert Giffard,
        Seigneur of Beauport.&nbsp; Through his wife he also inherits the
        seigneury of Fossambault.
@@ 34
Pierre's children would marry into the Imbeault, Cliche,
        Jobin, Rotureau and Sarrasin families.
@@ 35
Pierre dies on July 27, 1701, at the Hôtel-Dieu of Quebec, at
        the age of 46.
@@ 36
His widow remarries on January 10, 1705, in Charlesbourg,
        to Maurice Rhéaume.
@@ 37
Ignace</b></font>, born on January 17,
        1655, is baptized on August 29, 1655, in Quebec, several months
        after his twin brother.&nbsp;&nbsp; Why?&nbsp; Perhaps
        the twins were
        separated after birth, since their mother could not
        nurse them both.&nbsp; Ignace may have been put out to a wet
        nurse and baptized when he came back to the family.&nbsp; It seems a plausible hypothesis to me.
@@ 38
Ignace marries <b>Marie Bélanger</b> in Beauport on November 26, 1685.&nbsp;
        He is a shoemaker and farmer in Beauport.
@@ 39
Ignace has five children, all born in Beauport.&nbsp; They would marry into the
        Ferré, Petit, Laurent, Beaugis and Picher families.&nbsp; The youngest, born in 1698,
        would marry a Beaugis, a relative of his aunt, the wife of Pierre the
        Younger.
@@ 40
Ignace dies at 46, on October 6, 1701, at the Hôtel-Dieu of
        Quebec, a few months after his twin brother Pierre the elder.&nbsp;
        <i>Mathieu and Sébastienne's two twins die in the same year,
        less than three months apart
@@ 41
His widow remarries on November 9, 1705, in Beauport, to
        Jacques Parent.
@@ 42
Jean</b></font>, born on November 27,
        1657, is baptized in Quebec on December 9, 1657.&nbsp; His godparents
        are Jean Le Vasseur and Marie Lelart.
@@ 43
Jean marries <b>Claire Baucher</b> on October 30, 1684, at
        Ste-Famille, Île d'Orléans.&nbsp; In the marriage deed drawn up before
        the notaire Paul Vachon, Jean says he lives at St-Pierre, Île d'Orléans.
@@ 44
His eight children would marry into the Ouellet, Boissonneau, Lepage,
        Liénard,
        Lis, Roussin and Ricosse families.&nbsp; Some would live in Neuville, others in
        St-Vallier in Bellechasse.
@@ 45
Jean dies on June 6, 1699, still at St-Pierre, Île d'Orléans;
        he is 42..
@@ 46
Pierre the Younger</b></font>, born on
        January 21, 1662, is baptized in the Beauport chapel.
@@ 47
He marries there, at the same time as his brother Pierre the elder,&nbsp; on
        January 21, 1686, <b>Marie-Anne Beaugis</b>, born on January 27, 1669, in
        Quebec.&nbsp; They had signed a marriage contract before the
        notaire Paul Vachon.&nbsp; Their witnesses at the wedding are the notaire and Robert
        Choret, Pierre's eldest brother.&nbsp; The bride and groom cannot
        sign their names.&nbsp; Note that Robert himself would remarry a few days
        later, on February 7.
@@ 48
Pierre the Younger has eighteen children.&nbsp; They are all born in
        Beauport, but several would move to the Montréal area, where they would
        marry.&nbsp; They would marry into the Turcot, Brault, Chrétien,
        Roy, Huppé, Magnan , Maurier, Martin, Gauthier, Rapidiou, Larouche,
        Bouthillet, Trottier, Maheu and Taillon families.
@@ 49
Pierre the Younger dies on May 5, 1736, in Beauport, at the age
        of&nbsp; 74.&nbsp;&nbsp;With him dies the last member of the
        first generation of Chorets born in New France.&nbsp; Nearly
        ninety years had passed since his parents had settled there
        in 1647.
