Financed by Italian bankers in Lyon,
      another Italian (clearly all the captains of the day were
      Italian!), <b><font color="#000066">Giovanni da Verrazzano</font> </b>puts to sea for France in
      1524.&nbsp; He ignores the two usual routes (the North Atlantic or
      the Antilles) and heads straight for the island of Madeira.&nbsp; After two months
      he reaches what is now North Carolina.&nbsp;&nbsp; He too runs up
      against a coastline.&nbsp;&nbsp; So he decides to head north,
      hoping to find a passage.&nbsp; <b><font color="#000066">Magellan</font></b> found a passage
      at the southern tip of America in 1519; there must be one

      hope, but he is disappointed.&nbsp; He sails up rivers such as
      the Hudson, without success: the barrier across the route to Asia
      goes on and on.&nbsp; He gives French names to every place he visits,
      and spends two weeks taking on supplies at &quot;Refuge&quot;, which
      will later become Newport, Rhode Island.

      kilometres of coast he has found no way through to the Sea of Asia.&nbsp; But it was
      now known that between Florida and the &quot;new found lands&quot; the

A milestone in cosmography: we are
      in the presence of a <b><font color="#000066">New World</font></b>.&nbsp; With Verrazzano, the

      centuries: New France.&nbsp; Until 1760 this name would apply to
      everything France claimed or occupied in North America, from
      Hudson Bay to the Gulf of Mexico, from the Gulf of St. Lawrence to the &quot;Western
      Sea&quot; (a sea not yet discovered but suspected to
      exist).

@@ 32
In fact Jacques Cartier had
      brought back to France only quartz, not gold and
      diamonds.&nbsp; That was the end of his career.&nbsp; His <b> <font color="#000066"> desertion</font></b> was
      a formal act of disobedience to his superior, and did great
      harm to the colonizing effort.&nbsp; He was essential to keeping this
      first colony going: he alone knew the country and its people well; he
      had lived through two Canadian winters and knew the secret of the cure for
      scurvy.

      French or Indigenous, survive to this day.&nbsp; He opened
