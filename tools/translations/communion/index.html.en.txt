@@ title
First Communion
@@ 1
Seen by the Catholic Church as the essential
passage from childhood into social life, First Communion was a true
rite of initiation, weighty in consequences because of the commitments made
that day before, and toward, God and men.
@@ 2
First Communion crowned a year of catechism in which the children were taught
the Ten Commandments, Heaven and Hell, the sacraments, the deadly
sins… The ceremony usually took place on a
spring Sunday, around Easter.<span style="mso-spacerun: yes">&nbsp;
</span>At high mass, when it came time for
communion,&nbsp; the young<span style="mso-spacerun: yes">&nbsp; </span>communicants,<span style="mso-spacerun: yes">&nbsp;
boys on one side and girls on the other, </span>came forward solemnly in line to receive the consecrated host.<span style="mso-spacerun: yes">&nbsp;
Only then did the rest of the congregation come up to receive communion.&nbsp; There was </span>beautiful singing,&nbsp;<span style="mso-spacerun: yes">&nbsp;
</span>and white lilies and other spring flowers decorated the church.
@@ 3
As soon as the parish priest had announced the date of the ceremony, the mothers
hurried to the shops to buy fabric and sew (or have the
dressmaker sew) the outfits for the ceremony. The
little girls wore lovely white dresses and a veil of tulle or
lace.<span style="mso-spacerun: yes">&nbsp; </span>The little boys
wore a handsome brand-new suit, usually with short trousers.<span style="mso-spacerun: yes">&nbsp;
</span>They wore a white armband on the left arm.<span style="mso-spacerun: yes">&nbsp;
</span>For the occasion the children wore a new pair of shoes
for the first time.

A few days before the ceremony, the children went to confession and, for the first
time, received forgiveness for all the sins they had committed. With their souls purified
they returned home to families who watched over them
with great care: that state of purity had to be preserved
until the sacred moment when they would receive the Body of Christ.
@@ 7
There was no rest at home, because
after mass the family would be coming over. Out came the good dishes kept
for special occasions, and the embroidered tablecloths. Preparations went
full speed ahead for the festive meal, and a cake was ordered from the pastry shop,
sometimes a tiered one, topped with a little
figurine of a boy or girl communicant. People ate well and
laughed, but there was no dancing, out of respect for the religious nature of the
sacrament.

The young communicants received
gifts of a religious nature to mark this important day.<span style="mso-spacerun: yes">&nbsp;
</span>Most often these were a missal, little holy
statuettes,<span style="mso-spacerun: yes">&nbsp; </span>beautiful rosaries and,
above all, very elaborate holy pictures (lush gardens, arches of
light… depicting the earthly paradise).<span style="mso-spacerun: yes">&nbsp;&nbsp;
</span>The children<span style="mso-spacerun: yes">&nbsp; </span>slipped these
pictures into their missals, as bookmarks to remind them of the
solemn day of their First Communion.
@@ 11
On the
following Sundays it was not unusual to visit relatives, dressing the
child up again as a communicant.<span style="mso-spacerun: yes">&nbsp;
</span>That way, those who had not been able to attend the ceremony
could<span style="mso-spacerun: yes">&nbsp; </span>see the child in all
their finery and offer congratulations.
