Québec has
                a beautiful history.
@@ 21
You doubt it? Your school memories are of colons and habitants (settlers and farmers, two
              words that have become pejorative), and you
              feel like &quot;losers&quot; when you think of the
              Conquest, the Patriotes, the referendums...?
@@ 22
And yet, when you
              look at Québec's history from the other end of the
              telescope, with more distance, you find it beautiful. <b>It
              often takes someone from outside to tell us before
              we realize it. </b>Within five
              years I took two different training courses on
              the experience of immigrants and how they see
              Quebecers. Both trainers were immigrant women
              who had lived in Québec for many years and who, to better
              understand their adopted country, had studied our history (five
              years ago it was a Cuban woman and, recently, a
              Mexican woman).
@@ 23
What they said was the same:
              <font color="#000000"><span style="background-color: #F5F5F5"> &quot;<i>You have a beautiful
              history, a moving one, a story of the tenacity and survival
              of a young people. Rooted in
              French,&nbsp; and therefore European and Latin, soil, and&nbsp;
              later coloured by British and American influences, your
              history has made
              you something unique. Studying it made me love
              Québec and Quebecers even more
@@ 24
Let us forget the
              diehard Catholic and nationalist propaganda of our old history
              books.&nbsp; Let us look at the facts with good plain common sense. The first ancestors, those of the 17th century,&nbsp; showed admirable
              will and courage in choosing to settle in
              a country to which their way of life was not at all
              suited. But very quickly&nbsp; their ways and their
              outlook changed. They became different from the
              French who came here only to advance their
              careers. The English Conquest of 1759 is a good thing
                for the Canadiens, the French colony being on the brink
                of ruin at the time. Of course, the&nbsp; English would have liked
              to assimilate them, but, not being numerous enough and wanting the Canadiens on their side rather than on
              the side of the Americans, then in the midst of their revolution for
                independence, the English conquerors negotiated with
                the Canadiens,&nbsp; thereby allowing them to
                survive as a distinct people.
@@ 25
Ever since, the
              Canadiens, then the French Canadians, then the Quebecers, and
              now the French-speaking Quebecers have been marked by
              their knack for negotiating and making compromises in order to survive
              and grow.&nbsp;Québec's reality is changing
              quickly;&nbsp; the story continues....
