The history
        of Trois-Pistoles spans 300 years of settlement. Basque fishermen
        sailed up the river in the 16<sup>th</sup> century and landed on the Île
        aux Basques, that <em>&quot;jewel of the river&quot;</em>. Then its
        capricious currents carried Jean Riou to the shore of the
        Seigneury of Trois-Pistoles in 1696.
@@ 3
Trois-Pistoles
        and the neighbouring villages form the Regional County Municipality
        of Les Basques. The Île aux Basques lies 5 km
        off Trois-Pistoles; since 1929 it has belonged to the Provancher Society
        of Natural History of Canada, which made it a sanctuary
        for migratory birds. From 1990 to 1993, archaeological digs
        were carried out there under the auspices of Université Laval, prompted
        by historical discoveries in the archives of Bordeaux
        (France). These digs uncovered remains and
        various objects bearing witness to the Basques' main activity on the island
        at the turn of the 16th and 17th centuries: rendering the blubber of
        whales caught nearby.&nbsp;The digs
        also revealed traces of repeated stays by Indigenous peoples
        going back at least to the 8th century. There are signs suggesting
        that the Île aux Basques was the scene of the first trading
        exchanges between Europeans and Indigenous peoples in the
        St. Lawrence estuary. These discoveries earned the island
        recognition as a <i>National Historic Site</i> by the federal government
        in 2001.

THE BASQUES REGION
@@ 8
The
        Basques region is a vast territory comprising one town, ten
        municipalities and one unorganized territory. The town of
        Trois-Pistoles has the densest population but, on the other hand,
        covers only 7.74 square kilometres.
@@ 9
Unlike
        Trois-Pistoles, the unorganized territory of the Les Basques RCM
        has no year-round residents but covers a very large
        area: 115.57 square kilometres, or 10.23% of the
        total area of the RCM. This territory is used mainly for
        fishing, hunting and logging.
