THE ANCESTOR
        FROM THE VERY BEGINNING
@@ 2
Of
  all our ancestral lines known to date, Robert
  Caron is the first to arrive in New France, on <b> June 4, 1634</b> (he
  was in the first group brought over by Robert Giffard).
@@ 3
Mathieu Choret, also a settler in Beauport,
        near Robert Giffard's seigneury, would
  arrive only ten years later.
@@ 4
R</font></b><font size="3" face="Times New Roman">obert
  Caron, probably born on July 22, 1613, in
  Rouen, France, sails for New France on one of the four ships of the
  Compagnie Cheffault-Rozee (a company founded for 5 years, based in Rouen,
  and charged with carrying immigrants to the St. Lawrence valley). Robert Caron thus arrives with Robert Giffard, seigneur of Beauport, and all
  the first settlers who came with him: Jean Côté, Jean Bourbon, Noël Langlois, Zacharie Cloutier.
@@ 5
E</b>ach
  immigrant is hired for 3 years at 75 livres a year (with room and board). After that he is free to return to France or to settle in the country.<o:p>
  In 1636, Pierre Le Gardeur de Repentigny
  grants Robert Caron a piece of land at Longue
  Pointe, on the côte de Beaupré, near the Sault Montmorency (Montmorency
  Falls). Robert is an entrepreneur: in the registers of the
  notaires (notaries) of the time, we find deeds in his name for sales of timber for large sums and the sale of a lot in the town
  of Québec to the Ursulines (a lot &quot;ten toises by five
  toises&quot;, a toise being about two metres).
@@ 6
Marriage and family
@@ 7
O</b>n October 25, 1637,
  Robert marries Marie Crevet (aged 16), who had arrived in the country in
  July 1637. She is the daughter of Pierre Crevet and the late Marie
  Lemercier, of Bénouville, diocese of Bayeux, in Normandy.&nbsp; The wedding
  takes place in the chapel of Notre-Dame-de-la-Recouvrance. It is blessed by
  the Jesuit Charles Lallemant, who serves as parish priest in Québec. Jasmin
  Bourguignon, a friend, Noël Langlois, a&nbsp; settler from the very beginning, and Robert Giffard,
  surgeon and seigneur of Beauport, attend the wedding as witnesses. Seven children will be born of this union.
@@ 8
The church
                of Bénouville, in Normandy, Marie Crevet's home parish.
@@ 9
I</b>n
  1639 Marie Caron is born, and in 1641 Jean-Baptiste.
@@ 10
Note
  that in 1641 the population of New France&nbsp; is 300 inhabitants, whereas
  New England has 50,000.
@@ 11
I</b>n
  1642 Robert, fearing the Iroquois, not without reason, sells his land to his
  neighbour Guillaume Couillard for 150 livres. He settles in Québec
  on the côteau Sainte-Geneviève (Sainte-Geneviève hillside), later to be called «&nbsp;le Belvédère&nbsp;»;
  he clears land to make an opening and keep a better eye on the movements
  of the Iroquois. Timber is his main source of income. Several settlers of Norman origin had settled in this area, which is
  located where the present-day Collège des Jésuites stands, at the corner of
  René-Lévesque and Joffre.
@@ 12
F</b>rom
        the spring of 1643, he devotes himself to clearing his land on the heights
        of Québec. Three years later the Compagnie des Cent-Associès (Company of One Hundred Associates) gives
        him the official title to his grant of 40 arpents of land,
        between the properties of Louis Sédillot and Claude Larchevêque. Governor Louis D'ailleboust then gives him 20 more
        arpents. On December 6, 1652, Governor Jean de Lauzon confirms
        both titles, apparently to encourage him to stay there
@@ 13
I</b>n
  1647 Robert Jr. is born, in 1649 Catherine, in 1652
  Joseph and in 1654 Pierre.
@@ 14
F</b>or
        a reason known only to himself, in
  1654 Robert Caron sells all his properties in Québec. His land on the
        côteau Sainte-Geneviève is sold to Charles D'Ailleboust, the governor's
        nephew, for the considerable sum of 1700 livres. A few months
        earlier, Robert had bought from Julien Fortin dit Bellefontaine*, for
        500 livres, a farm five arpents wide by one league
        deep on the côte de Beaupré, at Sainte-Anne-du-Petit-Cap, about a mile east
  of the present-day&nbsp; basilica of Sainte-Anne.
@@ 15
I</b>n
  1656 Aymée Caron is born.
@@ 16
Julien Fortin dit Bellefontaine</a></u> is a direct ancestor of
  Marie-Hélène. But he is also our ancestor through the Caron line, since two
  generations of Carons married Fortins. So Julien, Olivier and
  Antoine Charette descend from Julien Fortin dit
  Bellefontaine through two lines, one maternal, the other paternal.
@@ 17
Death&nbsp; and remarriage
@@ 18
O</b>n
  July 8, 1656, Robert Caron dies at the age of 44 at the Hôtel-Dieu. An accident
        or an illness?
@@ 19
H</b>is
  death plunges Marie
  Crevet into poverty, her children being too young to
  support her.&nbsp; She splits up her
  family, sending the two oldest boys to friends in Québec, and
  she settles in Château-Richer.
@@ 20
O</b>n
        June 4, 1660, Marie Caron (the eldest daughter), who had married Jean
        Picard on July 28, 1656, is abducted by Hurons allied with the
        Iroquois, together with her daughter Louise (born September 29, 1659) and three settlers'
  children she
        was looking after. Her husband was sowing the fields with neighbours (people
  banded together
  to defend themselves better in case of attack).&nbsp;Alerted, Governor
  d'Argenson sends a party of French and Algonquins to search for
  the young woman and the children. They flush out the kidnappers at
  Pointe de Lévy two days later. Marie, lying in the canoe,
  hears friendly voices, raises her head and is struck by a bullet that
  also kills the child she is holding in her arms. She is taken
  to the hospital, where she
        dies two days later. Her little daughter is raised by her
  Picard grandparents.
@@ 21
O</b>n
  November 16, 1661, Jean-Baptiste Caron (the eldest son) marries Marguerite
  Gagnon. This allows Marie Crevet to return to her land at Sainte-Anne-de-Beaupré with her family.
  She houses and feeds
  Jean-Baptiste and his new wife, and in return he works the land
  and develops it.
@@ 22
In
1663 the population&nbsp; grows to 2,500 inhabitants ( and to 80,000 in
New England).
@@ 23
T</b>en
  years after Robert Caron's death, on
  July 27, 1666, Marie Crevet remarries, to Noël Langlois, in
  Château-Richer. To avoid quarrels between the Langlois and Caron
  heirs, Marie and Noël sign a marriage contract under
  &quot;séparation de biens&quot; (separate property). Marie and Noël have
  known each other for a long time. He was a pilot, born in France in 1603,
  who came to Canada in 1634 at the same time as Robert Caron. Noël had
  been a witness at Marie and Robert Caron's wedding. He himself had
  first married Françoise Garnier, with whom he had ten children.
@@ 24
In
1672 the population is 6,700 inhabitants (and 120,000 in
New England).
@@ 25
O</b>n
  July 15, 1684, Noël Langlois (stepfather of the Caron children) dies in
  Beauport at the age of 80.&nbsp; At that time he is the oldest&nbsp;
  inhabitant in the country.
@@ 26
I</b>n
  1695 Marie Crevet dies in Baie-St-Paul, where she lives with her daughter
  Catherine. Catherine, the widow of Jacques Dodier, had&nbsp; married
  secondly Pierre Dupré, who on December 30, 1682, was granted the
  seigneury of Le Gouffre. He is the first settler of Baie St-Paul.
@@ 27
SECOND
        GENERATION, JOSEPH CARON
@@ 28
J</b>oseph
  Caron,
  born in 1652 in Québec, settles in the Bas du Fleuve (the lower St. Lawrence). This is because in </o:p>
  the autumn
  of 1672 his younger sister, Aymée, marries one of their stepfather's sons, Noel
  Langlois fils dit Traversy. Frontenac grants him the
  Seigneury of Port-Joly. He will sell it in 1686, having been unable to meet
  the requirements expected of a seigneur, but in the meantime several of his
  Caron brothers-in-law, including our ancestor Joseph, follow their sister and
  her husband and move to this new region to be developed, the Bas du Fleuve.
@@ 29
I</b>n
  1678 Joseph Caron begins clearing land
  at Port-Joly. He lives with his brother Pierre at Cap St-Ignace. He is an excellent carpenter and builds several houses and barns
  for the settlers.
@@ 30
I</b>n
  March 1683 Joseph Caron builds the first <a href="capstignace.htm" target="_blank"> church of Cap
  St-Ignace.</a> It is made of wood and measures 40 feet long by 25 feet wide.<o:p>&nbsp;<br>
  On November 23, 1684, he marries Elisabeth
  Bernier at Cap St-Ignace.
@@ 31
I</b>n
  1711 Joseph Caron dies at Cap St-Ignace. He leaves 5 children.
