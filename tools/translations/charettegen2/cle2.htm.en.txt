The Filles du Roi (King's Daughters)
@@ 2
Between
        1663 and 1673, nearly 850 women left France to settle
        in America.&nbsp; They hoped in this way to escape miserable living
        conditions.&nbsp; The ladies in charge of recruiting them, moreover,
        told them that beyond the ocean lay a new country,
        big and beautiful, where every woman could have her husband, her house and her plot of
        land.&nbsp; How could they not be tempted? How could they turn their backs
        on a future that seemed so easy?&nbsp; Many girls were won over
        by the adventure and chose to set sail for the New World.

LOUIS
      XIV AND THE FILLES DU ROI
@@ 15
In 1663 King
      Louis XIV decided to take the little North American colony
      in hand.&nbsp; He found that <b><font color="#455287">it counted barely&nbsp; 3,035
      people, while the English colonies had around 75,000
      inhabitants</font></b>.&nbsp; 17th-century France was the
      most populous country in Europe, but people there believed that the more populous a country,
      the more powerful it was, since it was easier to raise an
      army.&nbsp; France did not want to lose population; that would
      remain its great fear until the 18th century.&nbsp;So the king wanted&nbsp;
      to encourage the local population to multiply as quickly as possible, but
      on its own.&nbsp; But how could that be done when the country had one marriageable
      woman for every six men?
@@ 16
The only solution was
        to <b><font color="#455287">encourage
        a large number of women to go and settle in New France</font></b>.&nbsp;
        So efforts were made to recruit girls in good physical health, fairly
        sturdy, not too unattractive and above all of childbearing age.&nbsp; They
        were given a dowry of 50 livres and promised a husband and a house,
        which was no small thing for girls for whom exile was a way to escape
        poverty.&nbsp; Then they were put aboard a ship bound for
        the New World.
@@ 17
The arrival of a ship was
        always an event in New France, and it drew even bigger
        crowds when it carried a convoy of young women.&nbsp; On the
        wharf were mostly the bachelor settlers who had come to meet
        their future wives, but also nuns and the families who were to
        lodge them.
@@ 18
From the very first weeks,
        the Ursulines arranged meetings between the bachelor settlers
        and the Filles du Roi.&nbsp; If a man was attracted to a young woman,
        he asked permission to court her and met her under the
        eye of a chaperone.&nbsp; <b>  <font color="#455287"> Courtship</font></b> often came down
        to a few walks in the town of Quebec.&nbsp; Before
        proposing, it was considered proper to offer one's beloved little
        gifts such as a hair comb or a shawl.
@@ 19
Generally, the men of
        the colony preferred women in their twenties, strong and well
        built, able to work the land.&nbsp; The Filles du roi, for their
        part, looked for a good match, that is, a man who already had
        a little money, land and a house already
        built.&nbsp; Love came second; what mattered was that their personalities
        got along, and they hoped the rest would follow.
@@ 20
Since the king's instruction was to keep
        courtship as short as possible, <i> couples married on average
        four to five months after they first met</i>.&nbsp; It is
        hardly surprising, then, that many girls regretted their choice and
        backed out at the last minute.&nbsp; Young men who went back
        on their promise had to pay their intended a certain sum
        in compensation.
@@ 21
These many marriages led to
        a <b> <font color="#455287"> certain mixing of social groups</font></b> and allowed a small number
        of women of modest condition to rise socially.&nbsp; In nearly
        one union in five, the age gap was more than 10 years.&nbsp; Most
        marriages took place in the autumn, at the end of the harvest. The ceremony
        was usually held on a Monday or Tuesday, and the celebration went on
        for two or three days.&nbsp; At that time it was out of the
        question to marry on a Friday or Saturday, since those were fast
        days.
@@ 22
Once the celebration
        was over, the women went to live on their husbands' land.&nbsp;
        They helped clear it, felling trees and pulling up
        stumps, and sometimes helped build the house.&nbsp;
        Usually they were pregnant within a month or two, which did not
        stop them from going on lifting stones and turning
        the soil.&nbsp; They had no respite, working from morning till
        night.

THE LEGACY
      OF THE FILLES DU ROI
@@ 25
The
        Filles du Roi played a great role in French settlement; it is
        estimated that <i> half of the French speakers of America have their blood</i>.&nbsp;&nbsp;
        Of the 850, 770 stayed
        (95%), some having died during the crossing and others
        having gone back to France, unable to adapt to the country.
@@ 26
From 1670 on the population
        grew so much that around 1678-1680 the number of people born
        here overtook the number of immigrants.&nbsp; What is more,
        the balance between men and women was restored.&nbsp; The Filles du roi were very
        fertile, with 9 children on average, while in France the
        average was 8.&nbsp; The population went from 3,000 in 1663 to 15,000
        in 1700.&nbsp; It must be said that natural selection played
        a part.&nbsp; The orphans from the Hôpitaux généraux, veritable
        breeding grounds for epidemics, had already passed a natural
        selection by surviving there.&nbsp; Another selection took place during
        the crossing.&nbsp; <i>Most of the Filles du roi lived longer
        than the women of France of the same period, and above all more
        freely

Marriage
        contracts in New France
@@ 28
Generally, unions were
        concluded quite quickly in New France.&nbsp; In the 17th century,
        a few months of courtship were enough for a man to ask for
        the young woman's hand.&nbsp; If she accepted, the <b> <font color="#455287"> betrothal</font></b>
        was arranged at the Church and the banns were published.&nbsp; To speed up their marriage,
        half of the Filles du roi obtained a dispensation from the publication of one
        bann out of three.&nbsp; As for betrothals, even though in 1698 the Bishop
        of Quebec banned them for fear they would encourage couples to
        live together before marriage, most of the Filles du roi, who came between
        1663 and 1673, were betrothed.
@@ 29
Though some Filles du roi skipped
        certain steps before marriage, few avoided
        the contract.&nbsp; It is estimated that about 80% of couples signed one.&nbsp;
        Before the notaire (notary), in the presence of friends, relatives and notables of the
        colony, the future spouses committed to community of property.
        Normally, the young woman contributed a dowry, larger or smaller
        depending on her social rank.&nbsp; We know, for example, that the Filles du roi
        received an amount ranging from 50 livres for those of modest
        condition to 100 livres for the demoiselles.
@@ 30
Anyone who could read and write
        could become a <b><font color="#455287">notaire</font></b>.&nbsp; He was appointed by the king to practise in
        a royal jurisdiction or by the seigneur to practise in a
        seigneury.&nbsp; So it was not necessary to have studied for years
        to do this job.&nbsp; It was mostly soldiers or
        craftsmen who became notaires.&nbsp; Some of them even became
        very important in the colony and left
        considerable archives.
@@ 31
The visit to the notaire did not,
        however, seal the union.&nbsp; The young woman was free to break
        the marriage contract whenever she pleased to marry another
        suitor, which happened in 11% of cases.&nbsp; Until her
        wedding day, then, the woman had the upper hand, and all she had to do was
        choose a good match.
@@ 32
<b> <font color="#455287"> Weddings</font></b> often took place in
        the autumn, after the harvest, since they could not
        be celebrated during Advent or Lent, nor in
        summer when the men were working in the fields.&nbsp; The wedding took place
        early in the week, on Monday or Tuesday, in the bride's parish;
        after the religious ceremony, everyone went in procession to the
        reception.&nbsp; On this occasion, relatives and friends gathered to
        celebrate.&nbsp; Sometimes even Governor Frontenac or
        Intendant Talon attended.&nbsp; It must be said that
        marriage was a very important event, since it ensured the survival of
        the colony.
