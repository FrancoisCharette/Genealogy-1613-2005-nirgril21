A series of undated postcards, of which we have nos. 2, 3, 4 and 6.  

These cards were made in Paris but do not date from the time when Charles was in Europe (1914-1919).  The  tone is more familiar than in the cards sent to his fiancée during the 1st war, and he talks about going to Boston.  But these cards are older than the ones from Kingston or Île-aux-Noix.  They were probably sent in the 1920s.

 

Card no. 2

       

 

Il c’est presentee contre langlais lauvrihier le tonguer de cuir.  Ma chère Noir je vous assure que je trouve le tin long est rien fair mais hier j’ai pris une resolution c’est que je veux aller est Boston pour le camoncement de l’autre moix

He ran against Langlais, the worker, the leather tonguer (?).  My dear Noir, I assure you the time drags, doing nothing, but yesterday I made up my mind: I want to go to Boston at the beginning of next month         

N.B. "Ma chère Noir" (my dear dark one) was a pet name for Ézilda, referring to her dark eyes (information from Jacqueline, who used to read Charles's cards and letters to her mother Ézilda)
